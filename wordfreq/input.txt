The Lighthouse Keeper

The keeper of the lighthouse woke before the sun. The sea was calm, and the wind was low. He climbed the stairs, all ninety of them, and checked the lamp. The lamp was old, but the lamp was reliable.

"Good morning," he said to the gulls. The gulls said nothing. They never did.

Every day the keeper wrote in his log: the weather, the tide, the ships that passed. Some days there were many ships. Some days there were none. On the days with no ships, he wrote about the sea instead - the colour of the sea, the sound of the sea, and the way the light moved over the water.

A storm came in the autumn. The wind was loud and the sea was wild. The keeper did not sleep. He stayed with the lamp all night, and the lamp stayed lit. In the morning, a ship sailed past the rocks, safe and slow. Its captain waved. The keeper waved back.

"Is it lonely?" a visitor asked him once. "Don't you want to leave?"

The keeper thought about it. "No," he said. "The sea is here. The lamp is here. And the ships need the light."

The visitor did not understand, but the keeper did not mind. He climbed the stairs again, all ninety of them, and checked the lamp.
